\section{Combat}

\subsection{Battle Field}

Combat is fought on a linear zone grid. \textbf{Z1} is the party rear (exit); \textbf{Z5} is the deep end of the fight.

\subsection{Opening}

Every fight opens the same way: decide who has \kw{Ambush}, \textbf{Deploy}, set \kw{Initiative}, then---if someone ambushed---resolve an \kw{Opening Rush}. Round 1 follows in Initiative order.

\paragraph{Ambush.}
At most one side has Ambush. If both would, \textbf{neither} does---go straight to a normal Initiative roll (with modifiers below).

\textbf{Party Ambush} if you have a Scout success for this fight (or an adventure/Omen grants Ambush) \textbf{and} one character passes a \textbf{Medium DEX} check (Aid allowed) to slip into position.
A character with the Echo Speech mutation (Campaign: Corruption Mutations) cannot grant or join a Party Ambush.

\textbf{Monster Ambush} if any of the following is true:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The adventure or sample fight says ambush (e.g.\ Campaign: Vein ambush).
    \item The chamber is a \textbf{Nest} or \textbf{Chokepoint} and you did \textbf{not} Scout this fight.
    \item The party is \kw{Exhausted} from empty Supply (Non-Combat) and you did not Scout this fight.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Warned.} If monsters would Ambush, one character may attempt an \textbf{Easy WIS} check. Success cancels Monster Ambush (standard Opening). Failure leaves it.

The side without Ambush is \kw{surprised} only during the Opening Rush (they do not act in it).

\paragraph{Deploy.}
Place figures before round 1. Without Ambush, the party places first, then monsters.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Party:} Put one character in \textbf{Z2} (front) and one in \textbf{Z1} (rear). Place the third in Z2 or Z1. Characters do not start in Z3--Z5 unless Ambush, an Omen, or an adventure says otherwise.
    \item \textbf{Monsters:} Use each monster's \textbf{Deploy} tag:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Front} $\rightarrow$ Z3, overflow Z4
        \item \textbf{Mid} $\rightarrow$ Z4, overflow Z3 then Z5
        \item \textbf{Rear} $\rightarrow$ Z5, overflow Z4
        \item \textbf{Swarm} $\rightarrow$ split evenly across Z3--Z4
    \end{itemize}
    \item A zone may hold at most \textbf{4} figures. Spill extras to the next overflow zone listed for that Deploy tag.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{If a side has Ambush:} the other side places first; the ambusher places second (they see the field).
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Party Ambush:} one character may start in \textbf{Z3}; the others stay in Z1/Z2.
    \item \textbf{Monster Ambush:} one \textbf{Front} monster may start in \textbf{Z2}; the others follow their Deploy tags.
\end{itemize}
Omens and adventure text that force starting zones still override.

\paragraph{Initiative.}
Initiative is set once and persists for the whole battle.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ambush:} the ambusher \textbf{auto-wins} Initiative (no roll).
    \item \textbf{No Ambush:} the party rolls \D{1}{6}; the monsters roll their encounter \textbf{Threat} die (printed on the lead monster or group; default \D{1}{6}; elites often use \D{1}{8}). Higher wins; ties go to the party.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Scout Edge:} if Scout revealed this fight but you have no Party Ambush, the party die is \D{1}{6}$+1$.
        \item \textbf{Exhausted:} if the party is Exhausted and not under Monster Ambush, the party die is \D{1}{6}${-1}$ (minimum 1).
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Party wins:} each round is Party phase, then Monster phase.\\
\textbf{Monsters win:} each round is Monster phase, then Party phase.

The Threat \textbf{die} decides Initiative when you roll. Threat \textbf{Points} (Campaign: Threat Budget) are the cost to put a monster in the fight. They share a name because both measure how much a creature is feared, but they are never added together. Ambush skips the roll entirely.

\paragraph{Opening Rush.}
If a side has Ambush, resolve this \textbf{before} round 1. Surprised figures do not act.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Party Ambush:} each standing character may take one free \textbf{Easy} Basic action---\kw{Strike} or \kw{Move} only. No Energy, no class/spell/relic actions, no pool roll.
    \item \textbf{Monster Ambush:} each monster resolves \textbf{only its first} action list entry, and only if that action is \easy. If the first entry is not Easy, that monster skips the Rush.
\end{itemize}

A character with Premonition active (Classes: Mage) ignores the first hit from a Monster Opening Rush.
Then begin round 1 in Initiative order with full phases.

\subsection{Hits, Energy, and Falling}

\paragraph{Hits.}
Each character has a number of \kw{Hits} equal to 3 plus their CON Attribute Dice (see Character Creation), usually 4--7. Mark one box each time you take a hit. Hits do not recover on their own; heal them with Campfire Counsel, class actions, or encounter rewards.

\paragraph{Energy.}
The party shares one \kw{Energy} track of \textbf{8} boxes. Every \medium{} \energy{1} action spends one. Energy refills to full at a Campfire and whenever you return to the surface. You regain \textbf{2 Energy} after every fight you win, and \textbf{1} for each Raw crystal you burn (Campaign: Aether-Glass Economy). Energy never exceeds 8.

\paragraph{Down.}
A character at 0 Hits is \kw{Down}: they contribute no dice, take no actions, and cannot be targeted while another character is still standing. If the party \textbf{wins} the fight, every Down character stands up with 1 Hit and takes a permanent \kw{Scar}: reduce their maximum Hits by 1 (never below 3). Write the Scar on the party sheet.

\paragraph{Broken.}
If all three characters are Down, the party is \kw{Broken}. You wake at the Gate mouth. Lose all unsealed loot, mark \textbf{Debt +2} with the faction nearest your Motivation, and advance the \textbf{World Crack Clock} by 1. If that clock was already full, the campaign ends in failure.

\subsection{Defense}

Defense is chosen in the Party phase, like everything else: you decide who stands in front, who Guards, and what they wear before the monsters roll. When a hit lands on a character, peel it through these layers \textbf{in order}; the first layer that can absorb it does.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Guard.} A character with \kw{Guard} ignores the first hit they would take before their next round, then loses Guard. A \textbf{Shield} makes Guard ignore two hits instead.
    \item \textbf{Armor.} Worn armor has \kw{Armor} boxes (Light 1, Medium 2, Heavy 3; see Equipment). Each box absorbs one hit once per fight. Boxes refresh at a Camp or in a Town, not between chambers.
    \item \textbf{Hits.} Whatever is left marks the character's Hits.
\end{enumerate}

Effects that say ``ignore Guard'' skip the first layer only; ``ignore Armor'' skips the second. ``Unavoidable'' hits skip both. A hit that is \textbf{redirected} to another character goes through that character's layers from the top.

\paragraph{Defensive actions.}
Actions marked \defensive{} in the class lists, the grimoire, and the relic tables are defensive: they grant Guard, redirect or cancel hits, restore Armor boxes, or heal. Every class has at least ten of them spread across its levels, so a party can be built to hold a chokepoint as readily as to burn one down. Defensive actions are assigned dice in the Party phase and last, unless they say otherwise, \textbf{until your next round}.

\paragraph{Heavy armor.}
A character in Heavy armor may \kw{Move} only once per round and cannot use actions that Move more than one zone. In a Static Field chamber or Static Rain, they take 1 hit at the start of every round unless Guarded---the Shard loves iron.

\subsection{Retreat}

A standing character in \textbf{Z1} may spend a \kw{Move} to leave the battle field instead of changing zones. The fight ends when every standing character has left; Down characters are carried out with them (they still take a Scar). Retreating from a delve has the costs listed under Campaign (lose unsealed loot, Debt +1).

\subsection{Targeting}

Monster actions often say ``one character'' or ``the nearest character''. Resolve them without a referee:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Nearest:} the standing character with the smallest zone distance.
    \item \textbf{One character} (in reach): the standing character with the \textbf{fewest current Hits}.
    \item \textbf{Ties:} prefer a character \textbf{without} Guard; if still tied, you choose.
\end{itemize}

Character actions that say ``one foe'' are always your choice among legal targets.

\subsection{End of Fight}

The fight is won when every monster is out. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Down characters stand with 1 Hit and a Scar.
    \item Regain 2 Energy.
    \item Clear Guard, Bound, Marked, and Burning from everyone. Spent Armor boxes stay spent until a Camp or Town.
    \item Collect Spoils (Equipment: Spoils and Shops) and any relic the chamber promised.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Dice Pool}

In combat you command a party of three characters. Each character has a \kw{Dice Pool} that grows with their level:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
  \shardheadercell{Level} & \shardheadercell{Pool} & \shardheadercell{Opens} \\
  \rowcolor{bone} 1--2 & \D{2}{6} & Easy, Medium, Hard Hard \\
  \rowcolor{ash!15} 3--5 & \D{3}{6} & Grand \grand \\
  \rowcolor{bone} 6--8 & \D{4}{6} & Dire \dire \\
  \rowcolor{ash!15} 9--10 & \D{5}{6} & Fell \fell \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

During the \textbf{Party phase}, roll \textbf{all party dice at once}: the sum of every standing character's pool, \D{6}{6} for a fresh party and up to \D{15}{6} for veterans. A character who is out of the fight contributes no dice, so the shared roll shrinks with every fallen companion.

\paragraph{Power actions.}
Actions marked \grand, \dire, or \fell{} want three or four dice from \textbf{one} character. They are the heavy blows, wide wards, and spells that separate a level-6 Ashblade from a hired knife. A character can never take more dice than their pool, so a Grand action is physically impossible before level 3, a Dire before level 6, and a Fell before level 9. Energy is paid once per action, as printed---never per die.

\subsection{Combat Round}

Each \kw{Combat Round}, resolve the two phases in Initiative order.

\paragraph{Party phase.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Roll:} Gather every active character's Dice Pool and roll them together.
    \item \textbf{Assign:} Place individual dice onto actions. A character may receive \textbf{at most as many dice as their pool} (2--5 by level). You may split those dice across that character's actions, but the total assigned to them must not exceed their pool, and all dice on a single action belong to the same character.
    \item \textbf{Resolve:} An action succeeds only if the assigned dice meet its difficulty (see Playing The Game: \easy, \medium{} \energy{1}, \hard{} \hard{}). Discard any unassigned dice for the rest of the phase.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Monster phase.}
Each active monster (or pack that shares one pool) acts using the Monster Phase rules below. Resolve monsters in zone order Z5$\rightarrow$Z1, and within a zone in the order they were deployed.

\begin{rpgbox}{Dice Pool Example}
    Your Warrior, Rogue, and Mage are all standing, so you roll \D{6}{6} and get
    $6$, $5$, $4$, $3$, $2$, $1$.

    The Warrior takes the $6$ and $5$ for \kw{Cleave} (\hard{} \hard{}).
    The Rogue takes the $4$ for \kw{Skirmish} (\medium{} \energy{1}).
    The Mage takes the $3$ for \kw{Ward} (\easy).
    The leftover $2$ and $1$ are discarded.

    No character received more than two dice.

    \textit{Three levels later} the same party rolls \D{9}{6}: $6$, $6$, $5$, $4$, $4$, $4$, $3$, $2$, $1$. The Warrior (pool 3) takes $6$, $6$, $4$---those faces would pay for a Dire action, but Dire picks start at level 6, so it is \kw{Cleave} plus a Medium \kw{Strike}. The Mage (pool 3) takes $5$, $4$, $4$ for a Grand spell. The Rogue takes the $3$ to \kw{Guard}.
\end{rpgbox}

\subsection{Monster Phase}

Monsters have their own Dice Pool, Hits, Deploy tag, Threat die, and an \textbf{ordered action list} (see Monsters). Actions are always read \textbf{top to bottom}---never reorder the list.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Roll the monster's full pool.
    \item Read actions from the top.
    \item For each action: if its condition is met (zone, range, target) \textbf{and} you can assign remaining dice to meet its difficulty, resolve it, spend those dice, then continue down the list with what remains.
    \item Skip actions that are illegal or unaffordable.
    \item Discard leftover dice.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{rpgbox}{Monster AI Example: Vein Tick}
    A Vein Tick (pool \D{2}{6}, Deploy Front) has this list:\\
    1.~Close In --- \easy: if not in a character's zone, Move toward the nearest character.\\
    2.~Bite --- \medium: deal 1 hit to one character in its zone.\\
    3.~Latch --- \hard{} \hard{}: deal 1 hit; that character cannot Move next round.

    It rolls $5$, $3$. Close In is skipped (already in Z2 with the Warrior). Bite spends the $5$ (Medium needs a face of 4+; monsters spend no Energy). Latch needs two 6s---skipped. The $3$ is discarded.
\end{rpgbox}

\subsection{Basic Actions}

Every character may use the following \kw{Basic Actions}, regardless of archetype.
Archetype-specific actions are listed under each archetype in Character Creation.

  \kw{Move} & \easy & Move to an adjacent zone on the battle field. \\
  \rowcolor{ash!15}
  \kw{Strike} & \medium{} \energy{1} & Deal 1 hit to one foe in your zone. \\
  \rowcolor{bone}

  \rowcolor{bone}
