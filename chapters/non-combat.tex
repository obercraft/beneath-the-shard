\section{Non-Combat Encounters}

Between delves and battles, Terramyr still demands a price. Five journey mechanics frame travel, Gate crossings, and camp.
Whenever a hex entered, a Gate, or a camp calls for a beat, roll \dper{} and open the matching entry in the companion volume \textbf{Beneath The Shard --- Journey Encounters} (\texttt{encounters.pdf}). The entries are grouped by mechanic: 01--20 Journey Clock, 21--40 Resonance Stress, 41--60 Debt \& Favors, 61--80 Shard-Gate Omens, 81--100 Campfire Counsel.

Every check outside combat is an \kw{Attribute Check} (Character Creation): one character rolls their Attribute Dice for the fitting attribute and counts 4+ as successes---Easy needs 1, Medium 2, Hard 3. Companions may Aid with \energy{1} per extra die, spent before the roll.

\subsection{Journey Clock (Supply)}
Travel is measured in \kw{Supply}: six boxes filled in a Town, one spent for every hex entered on the Map of Terramyr (Campaign: Hexploration) and one for every Camp. Each hex entered also draws one encounter. The Supply track \textbf{is} the Journey Clock the encounters speak of: ``clock $+1$'' means spend one more Supply, ``clock $-1$'' means regain one.

When Supply is \textbf{empty}, every further hex costs one price of your choice:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Late:} roll the next Gate Omen twice and apply both.
    \item \textbf{Lost:} resolve one extra encounter in this hex.
    \item \textbf{Exhausted:} the party rolls $-1$ die in its next fight; unless you Scouted that fight, monsters may gain Ambush (Combat: Opening).
\end{itemize}

Each such hex also marks $+1$ Resonance Stress. Camps and Towns refill Supply (Campaign: Hexploration).

\subsection{Resonance Stress}
Handling Aether-Glass fills the party's \kw{Resonance Stress} track of \textbf{6} boxes. Harvesting, carrying, and spending crystal marks Stress (Campaign: Aether-Glass Economy); so do Critical Misses and many encounters.
When the track fills, clear it and roll on the Corruption Mutations table in Campaign.
Reduce Stress through Campfire Counsel (Lore), Critical Successes, destroying crystal, and the rare costs listed in encounters.

\subsection{Debt \& Favors}
Marks track what factions owe you---or what you owe them. Keep one line per faction: the Mining Guild, the Crown, the Star-Cult, the Free Companies, and whoever else writes your name down.

\paragraph{Debt.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{3 Debt} with one faction: that faction sends collectors. Your next delve must include a \textbf{Collector chamber} (Threat 6: Debt Thug \#17 $\times$3 and Debt Collector \#56, or a faction-fitting equivalent at the same budget). Winning does not clear the Debt, but they stop coming for one Gate.
    \item \textbf{5 Debt} with one faction: \textbf{foreclosure}. The campaign ends in failure unless a Motivation beat explicitly offers another way out.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Favor.}
Burn one Favor mark with a faction to do one of the following:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Reroll every failed die of one Attribute Check (after seeing the roll).
    \item Pass one toll, checkpoint, or encounter involving that faction without resolving it.
    \item Clear 1 Debt with the \textbf{same} faction.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Shard-Gate Omens}
At the Gate, roll \D{1}{20} and apply entry \textbf{60 + result} from Journey Encounters for the whole delve. Omens twist the delve's rules (zones, Guards, who is Marked). Heeding an omen often costs Stress or gear; ignoring it invites worse timing in combat.
If the Journey Clock filled with \textbf{Late}, roll twice and apply both. A Campfire \textbf{Lore} success lets you see the Omen before you commit.

\subsection{Campfire Counsel}
Camp in any hex for 1 Supply, and at least once before each Gate (encounters may also grant a free camp). Energy refills to 8. Then choose \textbf{two} Counsel actions; each is attempted by a different character with one Attribute Check.
